I came across Mr. Oizo in the early 2000s, if I recall correctly. "Flat Beat" is a banger, and I kept coming back for more of his music. Maybe I'll write up my favorites someday.

Fast-forward to 2022 or 2023, when I rediscovered Mr. Oizo as Quentin Dupieux, a filmmaker making a ton of incredible movies. Here are my favorite Quentin Dupieux movies and some thoughts on them.
His movies have a sort of self-aware Wes Anderson feel without much pretension.
Most of them are silly, entertaining, and generally well executed—from the technical work to the writing and acting—all on impressively small budgets.
Nothing goes to waste in these movies, and it's incredibly refreshing.

## 1. Mandibles

Of all his movies, I think *Mandibles* is my favorite.
It's about two lowlife criminals who steal a car, find a gigantic fly in the trunk, and then get mistaken for old high school friends and invited to a vacation home.
It's silly and energetic, and I'm not sure why, but it reminded me of my youth.
It captures his filmmaking style without being too bizarre, while still being funny and surreal.
Highly recommended!

## 2. Yannick

*Yannick* is about a young construction worker who attends a play.
The play is boring, so he stands up and interrupts the performance to tell the actors they should make it better, offering some earnest and honestly good ideas.
The whole theater thinks he's insane and treats him that way.
Having traveled a long way to attend the play, only to be let down and disrespected, our protagonist hijacks the theater with a pistol, demands a typewriter and printer, and sets out to rewrite the play.
It has an incredible ending crescendo with a touching emotional high.

## 3. Smoking Causes Coughing

This movie is about a Power Rangers-inspired, monster-fighting team, except their superpowers somehow come from the deadly chemicals in cigarettes.
The movie is kind of a flex in filmmaking, pulling out and executing all the classic special effects, costumes, and gore.
We take many of these moviemaking techniques for granted now, so it's simply entertaining to see a movie almost explicitly devoted to performing these tricks well.
Beyond the technical filmmaking, the story follows a series of bizarre, funny, and generally entertaining tales.

## 4. Keep an Eye Out!

This movie follows a suspect and the cop interviewing him.
He is clearly innocent, but the evidence and circumstances keep stacking up against him.
It almost feels like a bad dream you can't wake up from.

## 5. Incredible but True

This is a funny movie about a couple who buy a house with a tunnel in the basement.
If you go down the tunnel, you end up climbing down a ladder from the attic, sort of like a portal.
The real estate agent shares something interesting about this portal, though: every time you go through it, you emerge six days younger, but two weeks pass for those outside it.
The wife, who is getting a bit older, immediately becomes addicted to the tunnel and seeks to regain her younger physique.
Meanwhile, her husband gets two weeks older every time she goes through. He ages considerably while she ultimately becomes only a few years younger.
The ending is shocking, and there are a slew of funny side stories, like the husband's obnoxious boss flying to Japan to get a “smart penis” augmentation that he can control with an iPhone app.
Between the cyberpunk subplots and the exploration of temptation and self-image, this is a great movie!

## 6. Wrong Cops / Wrong

I saw these when they came out, and I still really like them.
They are surreal and don't make much sense, but they're entertaining, and it's fun to watch the obnoxious characters develop on screen.
*Wrong Cops* is the better of the two.
*Wrong*, like *Rubber*, still has a lot of “no reason” all over it, which I don't enjoy as much. Still, the story about the psychic dog is really entertaining, and all the locations they shoot in are interesting on their own.
These movies feature a lot of released Mr. Oizo music, and it fits perfectly.

## 7. Steak

*Steak* is very bizarre.
It's also really funny.
It mixes a weird American letterman-jock aesthetic with a compulsive drive for plastic surgery, while everyone is obsessed with drinking milk and eating raw eggs.
The old lifted trucks they drive are really cool, too.

## 8. The Second Act

A bunch of funny dialogue between a group of great French actors.
It's layered over a surreal restaurant run by a waiter who can't pour wine without spilling it.
Simple, satisfying, and worth a watch.

## 10. Rubber

It's a movie about a killer tire.
Most of the movie is just full of “no reason.”
In a lot of ways, it seems like a filmmaking flex.
There are some funny scenes, but overall it's pretty slow, though it does have a few good songs.
